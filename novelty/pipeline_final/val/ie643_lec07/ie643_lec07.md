# Directional derivative:

For a fn. $f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$, the directional derivative along some **non-zero** direction $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$ at a point $x$ is defined as

$$f'(x; u) = \lim_{\alpha \downarrow 0} \frac{f(x+\alpha u) - f(x)}{\alpha}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page006 -->

# Gradient

Gradient of a fn at a point $x$

$$\nabla f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2} \\ \vdots \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_d} \end{bmatrix}$$

$$f'(x;u) = \langle \nabla f(x), u \rangle$$

$$u = -\nabla f(x) = -\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$$

If $\nabla f(x) \ne$ zero vector:
$$f'(x;u) = \langle \nabla f(x), -\nabla f(x) \rangle$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page007 -->

# Descent Direction

$$f'(x; -\nabla f(x)) < 0$$

**Descent direction:**

A non-zero direction $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is called a descent direction of $f$ at $x$ if:
$$f'(x; u) < 0$$

![A 3D surface with level curves (contours of $f$) labeled $w_1$, $w_2$, and an arrow pointing downward into the minimum, illustrating a descent direction.](figures/ie643_lec07_page007_b1.png){width=35.0836%}

# Property:

If $u$ (a non-zero vector) is a descent direction of fn $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ at $x$, then:

there exists some $\epsilon \ge 0$ such that for every $\alpha \in [0,\epsilon]$, $\lim_{\alpha\downarrow0}\frac{f(x+\alpha u)-f(x)}{\alpha}<0$

$$f(x+\alpha u)<f(x) \quad \Rightarrow \quad f'(x;u)<0$$

# Stochastic gradient descent

**parameter regime:** $10^6$, $10^8$, ...

$$D = \{(x^i,y^i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$$

$\to w^0$

$\to$ for $k=0,1,2,\dots$

$\hookrightarrow$ Compute a **stochastic gradient**

- Randomly choose $j\in\{1,2,3,\dots N\}$
- Compute $\nabla e(y^j,\hat{y}^j)$

$$w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + n_k(-\nabla e(y^j,\hat{y}^j))$$

Stochastic approx. $\Rightarrow$ convergence to the local opt/saddle pt.

might not lead to descent

# Mini-batch SGD

$$D = \{(x^i, y^i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$$

$\to w^0$

$\to$ for $k=0, 1, 2, \dots$

$\hookrightarrow$ Sample mini-batch of samples:
$$B_k \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$$

for every $j \in B_k$: compute $\nabla e(y^j, \hat{y}^j)$

$\hookrightarrow$ $w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + \eta_k \left(-\sum_{j \in B_k} \nabla e(y^j, \hat{y}^j)\right)$

$\hookrightarrow$ convergence in asymptotic sense

# Forward Chain Rule

![A chain of neurons — input layer neuron → $j^{th}$ neuron in layer $l-1$ → $i^{th}$ neuron in layer $l$.](figures/ie643_lec07_page017_b1.png){width=55.0000%}

$i^{th}$ neuron in layer $l$, $j^{th}$ neuron in layer $l-1$: $w_{ij}^l$

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial w_{ij}^l} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_i^l} \cdot \boxed{\frac{\partial z_i^l}{\partial w_{ij}^l}}$$

forward chain rule:
$$z_i^l = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{l-1}} w_{ik}^l a_k^{l-1}$$
$$a_i^l = \phi(z_i^l)$$
$$= \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_i^l} a_j^{l-1}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page018 -->

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial z^l_i} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial a^l_i} \left(\frac{\partial a^l_i}{\partial z^l_i}\right) = \frac{\partial e}{\partial a^l_i} \cdot \phi'(z^l_i)$$

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial a^l_i} = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z^{l+1}_m} \cdot \left(\frac{\partial z^{l+1}_m}{\partial a^l_i}\right) = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z^{l+1}_m} \cdot w^{l+1}_{m,i}$$

![A neuron $a^l_i$ (layer $l$ input) feeding into multiple neurons of layer $l+1$, labeled $z_m^{l+1}$ and $a_m^{l+1}$.](figures/ie643_lec07_page018_b1.png){width=39.5414%}

# Chain Rule

$$z^{l+1}_m = \sum_{k=1}^{N_l} w_{mk}^{l+1} a_k^l$$

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial a_i^l} = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$

$$= \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial a_m^{l+1}} \cdot \frac{\partial a_m^{l+1}}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$

$$= \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial a_m^{l+1}} \phi'(z_m^{l+1}) \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$
